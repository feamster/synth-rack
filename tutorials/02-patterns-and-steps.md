# Patterns and per-step changes

A track's pattern is the row of 16 step keys. A lit key is a note (a "trig"). This tutorial is
about making individual notes differ from each other, which is what makes a pattern sound played
rather than stamped out.

## The red outline

- **Left-click** a step key: add or remove a note.
- **Right-click** a step key: *hold* that note for editing. It gets a red outline.
- Right-click it again, or press `Esc`: let go.

While a step has the red outline, the knobs change **that one note only**. With no outline, they
change the whole track.

## Give one note its own pitch, loudness, length or chance

The four small knobs are per-note settings:

| Knob | What it sets for the held note |
|---|---|
| NOTE | pitch |
| VEL | how hard it is hit |
| LEN | how many steps it lasts (matters for bass and lead sounds) |
| PROB | the chance it plays each time round; below 100% the pattern varies by itself |

Try it on Forge: press `7` (bass), right-click a lit step, turn NOTE up a few semitones, press
`Esc`. That note is now a different pitch from the others.

With no step held, the same four knobs set what a *new* note gets when you click an empty key.

## Give one note its own sound (parameter locks)

The big knobs work the same way. Hold a step, turn CUTOFF: only that hit gets brighter or darker.
The knob's ring turns red and the step shows a red dot, so you can see what is locked. To remove
a lock, hold the step and double-click the knob.

Things worth trying: open the filter on one bass note per bar; pitch one snare hit down with TUNE;
give every fourth hi-hat a longer DECAY.

## Record instead of clicking

Press `R` (or the REC pad) while the pattern plays, then play the pads (`1`–`8`) or the piano
keys. What you play is written into the pattern on the nearest step. Fix individual notes
afterwards with the red outline.

## Longer patterns, more patterns

- STEPS sets the length: 16, 32, 48 or 64. The four small lamps above the step keys are pages of
  16; click one, or use `←` `→`.
- PATTERN switches between eight patterns (A1–A8). While playing, the change waits for the end of
  the current pattern, and both drum machines switch together.
- Right-click the module for *Copy Pattern* and *Paste Pattern*.
